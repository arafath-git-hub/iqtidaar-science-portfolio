KNOWLEDGE

THE SUN

Type: G-type main-sequence star (Yellow Dwarf)

Age: ~4.6 billion years

Diameter: 1,391,000 km (109× Earth)

Surface Temperature: ~5,778 K

Significance: The Sun is the gravitational center of our solar system, providing energy and heat that sustains all life on Earth.

THE MOON

Type: Natural satellite of Earth

Diameter: 3,474 km (27% of Earth)

Distance from Earth: ~384,400 km

Orbital Period: 27.3 days

Significance: The Moon influences Earth's tides, stabilizes our planet's axial tilt, and has been humanity's first destination in space exploration.

STARS

Definition: Massive, luminous spheres of plasma held together by gravity

Classification: Hertzsprung-Russell diagram classifies stars by temperature and luminosity

Life Cycle: Main sequence → Red Giant → White Dwarf (or supergiant phases)

Significance: Stars are the primary sources of light, heat, and heavy elements in the universe.

GALAXIES

Definition: Massive systems containing billions to trillions of stars

Types: Spiral, Elliptical, Lenticular, Irregular

Our Galaxy: The Milky Way is a large barred spiral galaxy ~100,000 light-years across

Significance: Galaxies are the largest gravitationally-bound structures in the universe.

MERCURY

Smallest planet, closest to the Sun. Extreme temperature variations due to lack of atmosphere.

VENUS

Hottest planet with a dense, toxic atmosphere. Rotates backwards relative to other planets.

EARTH

Our home. The only known planet to harbor life. Liquid water and protective magnetic field.

MARS

The Red Planet. Evidence of past water. Future target for human space exploration.

JUPITER

Gas giant with the Great Red Spot. Largest planet in our solar system. 79+ known moons.

SATURN

Iconic ringed planet. Second-largest planet. 83+ known moons including Titan.

URANUS

Ice giant with extreme axial tilt (98°). Rotates on its side relative to orbit.

NEPTUNE

Windiest planet. Deepest blue color. 14+ known moons. Furthest from the Sun.

NEBULAE

Giant clouds of gas and dust where stars are born. Can emit, reflect, or absorb light creating stunning visual displays.

BLACK HOLES

Regions of spacetime with extreme gravity where nothing, not even light, can escape. Result from massive star collapse.

SUPERNOVAE

Extremely energetic stellar explosions that briefly outshine entire galaxies. Create and scatter heavy elements.

COMETS

Icy bodies that develop tails when approaching the Sun. Ancient messengers from the outer solar system.

SOLAR FLARES

Sudden, intense bursts of electromagnetic radiation from the Sun. Can affect Earth's magnetosphere.

AURORAS

Natural light displays in Earth's atmosphere. Result from solar wind interactions with magnetic field. Northern/Southern Lights.
